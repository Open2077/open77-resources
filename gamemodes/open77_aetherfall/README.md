# Aetherfall — The Astral Conclave

An in-progress private demonstration of Open77: a multiplayer mage arena with
server-owned spells, aether, shields, elimination rounds and a dedicated WebUI.
This is a working gameplay foundation, **not the finished visual demonstration**.

## Run locally

From the repository root, with the matching development Open77 client and
the casting, original prop and NCWE world preload packages installed (including
the map's ArchiveXL loader dependency):

```powershell
pwsh -NoProfile -ExecutionPolicy Bypass -File scripts/agent-play.ps1 up -StartServer -DevLocal -ServerConfig server.aetherfall.jsonc
pwsh -NoProfile -ExecutionPolicy Bypass -File scripts/agent-play.ps1 connect -DevLocal -ProcessId <first-client-pid>
pwsh -NoProfile -ExecutionPolicy Bypass -File scripts/launch-extra-client.ps1 -IdentityProfile aetherfall-observer -PlayerName Vesper
pwsh -NoProfile -ExecutionPolicy Bypass -File scripts/agent-play.ps1 connect -DevLocal -ProcessId <second-client-pid>
```

The profile is loopback-only, uses its own resource-cache directory, and selects
one gamemode. It starts this otherwise optional resource with `ensure`. Do not
enable Freeroam/Pursuit/Deathmatch spawn ownership alongside it. Native archive
changes require restarting the game. The casting resource contains the private
preload ZIP; a launcher installs it before starting Cyberpunk.

Spell effects: Emberbolt and Astral Lance fly Hogwarts Legacy's Flipendo and
Petrificus Totalus (projectile and impact), converted and cooked by NCWE Studio
into the preload package of `open77_aetherfall_vfx` (resources/assets/maps,
git-ignored: private). The paths are in `shared/config.lua`; the package's
README says how to choose others. A client started without the launcher needs
that package's `archive/pc/mod` installed in the game by hand, as the others.

Spell sounds: Hogwarts Legacy sound events, mixed as they play there and
converted to mono WAV in `audio/hogwarts/` (excluded from git in
`.git/info/exclude`: private), chosen in `shared/sounds.lua` (generated by
`scripts/aetherfall/HogwartsVfx/aetherfall_sounds.py`, whose table says which
event plays at which moment). The server plays one variant per moment: cast,
flight (follows the projectile), impact (projectile hit, area eruption), area
warning, shield on, deflection when a shield absorbs a hit, shield end,
elimination; each handle is released after its clip (16 per resource).

Training grounds and duels: the arena exists twice, in two routing buckets.
Players arrive on the **training grounds** (bucket `lobby.bucket`, 12771): no
rounds, every spell works at once, mages never harm each other and spells
pass through them; they strike the **training dummies**, passive and silent
server NPCs (`training` in `shared/config.lua`: positions, health, one
walking a patrol to practise leading shots) whose health is the mode's: they
fall, then rise again after `respawnMs`. The **Conclave menu** (`N`, keyboard
and mouse while open, Escape closes it) joins or leaves the duel queue,
starts a duel once `duel.minPlayers` mages are queued (any of them may start
it), raises the dummies, or leaves a running duel. A duel takes the queued
mages into the duel bucket (12770) for the usual rounds; when it is won, or
cannot go on (a mage left), everyone returns to the training grounds.

Ready players on foot enter automatically. `/aether join` retries entry,
`/aether leave` returns to the saved position/bucket and releases the camera,
HUD and input claims, and `/aether status` prints the caller's rules state.

Two to eight players fight free-for-all rounds. Last surviving mage earns one
victory; first to three wins the match. A timed-out round is a draw. Placements
must settle before countdown, and eliminated players watch until the next round.

| Input | Spell | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| Left click | Emberbolt | 22 damage; free; 0.65 s recharge |
| V | Astral Lance | 48 damage; 25 aether; 4.5 s recharge |
| E | Cinder Nova | 42 area damage; 0.85 s warning; 30 aether |
| R | Prismatic Ward | Absorb 65 damage for 2.2 s; 20 aether |
| F | Starfall | 90 area damage; 1.7 s warning; 70 aether |

Bindings can be changed in Open77's key settings. Lance defaults to V, which
avoids both AZERTY strafe-left and Cyberpunk's native right-click aim toggle.
The HUD displays the configured bindings, including its round-start reminder.
H toggles the spellbook with descriptions, costs and recharge times. K toggles
the match standings with wins, kills/deaths, damage and participant status.
Press the same key again to close, or switch directly to the other panel.
The duel continues while a panel is open; close it before casting.

## Ownership and synchronization

`server/duel.lua` is a deterministic rules model. Clients submit only a spell
identifier, direction and increasing intent sequence. Position comes from the
server's accepted player snapshots; it is not a client event field. The server
owns all costs, cooldowns, capsule sweeps, area expiry, shields and score.

`server/main.lua` uses generic platform APIs for readiness, travel, routing,
freeze claims, replicated effects and spatial package audio. The Lua rules do
not write REDengine memory. The 20 Hz simulation sends state to the HUD at
10 Hz. A stalled simulation retires outstanding attacks instead of applying
retroactive hits to current positions. Late participants wait for the next
round. Native damage involving an arena participant is vetoed; this mode has
its own health ledger and currently presents elimination with a freeze.

The native core now supports generic transient clips from a selected locomotion
package. The server owns playback, and native sampling blends the imported pose
without root motion or game events. No Aetherfall identifiers or spell-specific
branches belong to the native core.

## Verification

```powershell
python scripts/aetherfall/check-rules.py
node scripts/aetherfall/check-hud.cjs
```

The rules runner uses Python `lupa`'s Lua 5.4 runtime. The HUD runner uses Node
Playwright (`OP77_PLAYWRIGHT` may name an installed package path). It loads the
production CEF bridge, substitutes only its native transport, checks four
viewport sizes and writes screenshots to `artifacts/aetherfall/`.

October 17 checkpoint: 19 rules, six presentation and 63 browser checks pass. Two actual
Cyberpunk clients verified entry, shared rounds, projectile damage, Starfall
damage, ward absorption, elimination, next-round reset and explicit return.
See [the research record](../../../docs/research/aetherfall-arena.md) for the
exact evidence and what that run does **not** prove.

The mode requires both `open77_equipment` and `open77_wardrobe`: their client
handlers complete the canonical appearance records needed to spawn remote
puppets. The initial minimal server profile omitted them. Earlier damage/HUD
checks therefore do not establish visible opponent rendering. The manifest and
profile now include them explicitly. `/appearance.status` reports whether the
server holds body and presentation records for the caller's visible peers.

Open `web/index.html?preview` in a browser for the labelled HUD-only art preview.
Preview data is never enabled when the real Open77 bridge exists.

## Assets and remaining scope

The five PCM sounds are original synthesized source assets, reproducible with
`python scripts/aetherfall/build-audio.py`. Native VFX currently use Open77's
existing Cyberpunk aliases. Five Hogwarts casting/shield motions have now been
decoded and retargeted to both Cyberpunk rigs. All ten native banks and the
four package graph/entity variants pass offline round-trip checks. This private
package is `resources/examples/open77_locomotion_aetherfall`; it must not be
published. `scripts/aetherfall/build-animation-banks.ps1` reproduces the bank
bake from local tool checkouts and the installed source game.

Casting uses the generic `Open77.locomotion.playClip`/`stopClip` server API.
See [the package API](../../../wiki/custom-locomotion.md). Local and opponent
views now verify imported motion on both body families, including a return to
native idle. Complete clip/graph/movement coverage and final presentation
acceptance remain open.

Permanent scenery is authored through the NCWE MCP. The editable observatory
project is `E:/Open77/Maps/Aetherfall/Aetherfall.ncwe`; its exported resource is
`resources/assets/maps/open77_aetherfall_world`. The floor centre is
`(2570,113,96)`, with a 19 m gameplay radius. The map has its own floor/wall
collision, nine lights and an interior atmosphere/reverb volume. A separate
`open77_aetherfall_assets` package registers original wand/sigil meshes through
the generic `Open77.props.registerModel` API. The server owns their gameplay
lifetimes. Source Blender and material scripts live in `scripts/aetherfall/`.
NCWE export 1.0.1 is installed locally and rendered in two actual clients.
Sampled floor contact, local/remote Nova warning circles and fire lighting,
wand replication and prop cleanup on explicit leave are verified. The research
record links the foreground captures and identifies the remaining coverage.

Wand attachment now waits for a fresh parent snapshot and retries on arrival;
a fresh solo waiting room and subsequent second-client entry verify it.
Its wrist-relative offset is now derived from the imported curled-finger pose;
local and close opponent captures verify coarse placement during Lance/Nova.
Exact finger contact and the male grip remain under review.

Two temporary duelist costumes use `open77_appearance` 0.2.0 exports and are
visible to both players. They preserve the original equipment and all saved
wardrobe outfits. A generic wardrobe attachment repair passes three live
join/leave cycles with an active saved outfit; the actual garment appearance,
not just its registry, is checked after restoration.

The original Prismatic Ward shell uses a generic player-root prop binding.
Its lifetime follows the authoritative shield, including depletion, expiry,
round end and leaving. Two clients verify local movement, matching observer
placement, expiry and removal on leave. A live Lance/Emberbolt exchange depletes
65 shield points, deals the remaining 5 HP and removes the shell before expiry.

Required next work includes final hand grip/costume art and male costume coverage,
movement-aware casting and impact reactions, richer spell VFX,

late-join/reload
verification, and final two-client visual/performance acceptance. Stopping the
