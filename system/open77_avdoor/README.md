# open77_avdoor

This resource gives every AV (the flying vehicles) the door the base game never had: walk up to a parked aircraft, press F, and your body turns to its door and steps up into it -- then you are in its pilot's seat, or a passenger seat when someone else may fly it. Stepping out puts you on the ground beside the door instead of under the hull, the doors swing for everyone, an AV coming down over one of the game's 208 AV pads is set down on its centre, and a MaxTac AV that a player flies plays its own take-off, descent and touchdown sounds.

- **Docs:** [Network vehicles](https://open2077.net/docs/vehicles)
- **Starts:** on its own
- **Needs:** `open77_interactions`; `requiredMods.enabled = true` for the Excalibur's door (a preload archive the launcher installs)
- **Runs on:** server and client

## Use it

List `open77_avdoor` after `open77_interactions` in `resources.load` and it works for every aircraft the server spawns. It owns no vehicle: it only seats players in hulls that already exist, through the platform's own `warpPlayerIntoVehicle`.

**The door.** The server measures every player on foot against every aircraft in their routing bucket (every 500 ms). When a parked aircraft is within 9 m and its door is open to that player, the player sees a card on the hull: *Fly the aircraft* or *Ride along*, key F. The press is judged again from scratch on the server (distance, speed, lock, seat, life), the seat's door swings open, and the body steps in about half a second later. An aircraft that appeared less than 2.5 s ago is boarded once it has settled: a pilot seated sooner flew a hull other screens still drew on its pad.

**Who may fly what**, in this order:

1. a rule another resource put on the hull (`allow`, below): the pilots it names fly, everyone else may ride along unless the rule closes the passenger seats;
2. otherwise the lock: a hull locked for a player is shut to them, as a car is;
3. otherwise `PILOTS` in `shared/config.lua`: `'anyone'` (the default) flies an unlocked aircraft; with `'registered'`, only pilots a resource named fly and everyone else rides along (`PASSENGERS`).

**The exit.** The platform drops a pilot inside the hull (its deferred eject, which once put the body under the hull, no longer reaches the local player: no Staging exit logged one). While the body sits in an aircraft the client reads its seat every frame; on the frame the seat is let go it stands the body on the ground beside its seat's door (the other side, behind or ahead when there is no ground there, a roof's edge for example) and puts it back if anything moves it -- with the image on: the screen is never cut to black at the exit (see **The curtain**). The server opens that door for everyone and closes it nine seconds later, or once the exit clip is over. For the airframes whose doors are known (`AIRFRAMES`: the Rayfield Excalibur, whose one door is on the left, so every seat leaves on the left; the Militech Manticore's two sliding doors, one a side), the real door is used.

**The Excalibur's door.** The platform opens a cabin door through the game's quest door event, which changes the door's state but never moves its mesh on the hulls the Open77 client draws. A trunk it opens by sending the animation feature and event itself, and that works. So this resource ships the Excalibur's animation graph with its one door listening to the trunk (`dist/Open77AvDoor_<rev>.zip`, installed by the launcher before the game starts; built by [`scripts/avdoor`](../../../scripts/avdoor/README.md)), and swings `trunk` for every Excalibur seat: its upper wing goes up and its middle panel folds out into a step. A game started before the archive was installed keeps the old graph until it restarts.

**The Manticore's doors and its exit key (1.9.0).** The Militech Manticore -- Rosalind Myers' AV, whose door her boarding and exit clips were made at -- has a sliding door on each side, two panels that slide apart (1.9 s, open at 1.07 s). Its graph is the game's four-door car graph, each door entered by its own events, and the platform's cabin-door path moves none of them: on Staging the boarding step played against a shut door. The archive now also holds its graph with the left door's events swapped with the trunk's and the right door's with the hood's (the Manticore has neither), so the left seats swing `trunk` and the right seats `hood`. The game's own exit from its seats is a car's, animated: the body is held mounted in the exit's workspot for two seconds, which no placement beside the door survives (eight in 1.5 s, the clip given up, the screen jumping). So while a body sits in a Manticore (`AIRFRAMES` ... `EXIT_FORCED`), F is this resource's (`LEAVE`, `consume`, dropped once the body is out): the press asks the server, which checks the seat and steps the body out through the platform's forced exit, instant on the leaving game, and the exit is the Excalibur's -- the door slides open, the body is put beside it and steps down out of it. A game that cannot consume the key, or an exit made another way (a game pad), keeps the game's own animated exit: then nothing is placed and no clip is played. The boarding closes the sliding door as its clip ends (`DOOR_CLOSE_LEAD_MS = 0`), before the seat.

**On its gear.** The platform's flight sets an AV down on the lowest point of its collision hull: the Excalibur then floats with its gear 0.4 m off the ground and its door's step 1.1 m up. Once the last body has stepped out of an airframe whose gear is known (`AIRFRAMES` ... `LAND`), the server lowers the hull onto its gear (`setTransform`, before the exit clip is asked), on the ground the exiting player's game measured under each foot -- one floor under all of them, coming down by 5 cm to `MAX_DROP_METRES` (3.0 m for the Excalibur since 1.7.9; 0.8 m before), nobody aboard, nobody freezing it. A set-down of more than `EXIT.LAND_GLIDE_FROM_METRES` (0.5 m) is glided rather than made in one move (1.7.9, below). `EXIT.LAND = false` leaves every hull where the flight put it.

**The exit, animated.** With [open77_avexit](../open77_avexit/) running, the body steps out of the door the way Rosalind Myers leaves her AV in Phantom Liberty: the server gives each player the `avexit` locomotion style while they sit in an aircraft, the client stands the body where the clip ends (turned so the clip's first frame is on the door), lets it land there, holds it once it stands, brings the curtain down, asks for the clip under it, and the server plays it for everyone at once; the image comes back on the first step and the body is held still until the clip ends. The body is put on the ground where the clip ends (3 cm up, `LIFT_METRES`), so the clip ends standing on it. Every other game sees nothing of the platform's own exit (the drop in the cabin, the eject under the hull): the body is hidden from them until the clip starts. A player whose style another resource gave, a hull in the air, or a server without open77_avexit: the plain exit above. `EXIT_ANIM = false` switches it off.

**The curtain.** A native quest fade (`Open77.screen.fadeOut`) holds the player's body still wherever it is, for as long as it is out: the platform's own notes say so of every vehicle (the fade's byte is world-update flag 0x8, in the 0x1B physics block), and the laptop showed it of the player -- up to 1.5.3 the exit cut to black at its first frame and put the body beside the door under it, where it hung 3 cm over the ground, "off the ground (falling)", 0.00 m/s, for the whole 5.4 to 7.5 s of black, whatever was done to it; the platform plays no clip on a body off the ground, and the body fell the moment the image came back (the client's own "first move" rule fired exactly then in each exit: Staging, 2026-10-28, 01:54:23.2, 01:54:39.2, 10:33:02.7). So since 1.6.0 the exit is never black at its first frame -- the client hides the pilot's body for 1.6 s and its chase camera holds the AV in view for 1.5 s while the body is put beside the door and lands -- and the screen goes black (`EXIT_FADE.OUT_MS`, 250 ms) only once the body stands there, faces the step's heading and is held, to cover the cameras' change to third person; nothing moves the body while it is down -- a move that has to be made lifts it first (`LIFT_MS`, at once), the body lands, and it comes down again once the body stands (`TRIES` 2 an exit) -- and it comes up with the step's first frame (`IN_MS`), or at `MAX_MS` (6 s), when the step plays in view. A plain exit (no clip) is not faded at all. `EXIT_FADE = false`: no curtain.

**The head.** About 1.5 s after an AV exit the Open77 client shows the pilot from behind through its legacy camera path, which draws the game's first-person body, the one with no head. From the moment the seat empties the client holds the platform's own third person instead (the dressed stand-in body, with its head -- on this screen the only body the clip plays on) until the clip ends, or, without a clip, until the player walks off. The seat being left, which the platform still reports for a moment, does not end the hold. The client's chase camera (17 m behind the hull, until its own exit deadline) and then its legacy camera (2.5 m behind the body) keep the platform's third person out -- cameras detached under the perspective arbiter's first-person decision, answered with its fatal row -- so the exit tells the two apart by where the camera stands (`Open77.camera.view`), leaves the chase camera to the client and turns the legacy one off on the first look that sees it (`LEGACY_VIEW_OFF`), never once the stand-in is drawn. The arbiter then lets third person in after twelve clean FRAMES and reveals the stand-in: the clip is asked the moment it is drawn, under the curtain, with the body held where the clip ends -- about 2.5 s after the exit at 60 frames a second, 6 s at 3 -- and a view that never comes is given up after `VIEW_WAIT_MS` and `VIEW_WAIT_LOOKS` watch passes since the legacy camera went off, or at `VIEW_WAIT_MAX_MS`. Every answer of the arbiter during the exit is a line in the client log (`exit view: the platform's perspective: ...`).

**The shot.** As the stand-in is drawn, before the image comes back, the camera swings `ORBIT_DEGREES` (160) round the body (`Open77.camera.orbit`, the platform's preview orbit): the clip is seen from in front, the body stepping down out of the open door toward the lens; it swings back behind the body as the clip ends. The server holds the door open through the clip and `DOOR_AFTER_CLIP_MS` after it. A swing seen turning the body with it is let go of, and that client makes no swing again until it restarts.

**The step's heading.** The clip's whole path turns with the body: a body facing the wrong way starts its first step metres from the door, in the air, walking toward the AV (Staging, 2026-10-28: planned facing 75 degrees, played facing 245.8 -- "walking out in the air nowhere near the av door"). So the client watches the body's heading (`Open77.character.yaw`) from the moment it is placed until the clip ends, and writes every turn to its log with what was going on (the client's cameras, the swing, the hold). Before the clip is asked, a body more than `FACING_DEGREES` (4) off is turned back where it stands (`FACING_TRIES`, 3) and must hold the heading for two looks; while the ask is in flight and as the clip starts it is read again and turned back. A body that will not stay turned plays no clip: it is shown standing beside the door, never walking the wrong way.

**On its feet.** The platform draws the clip only on a body whose own game says it stands on the ground -- on every screen, from that one sample (the stand-in here, the copies elsewhere). With the image on, a body put beside the door lands there: it must stand two looks in a row, face the step's heading (turned back first if not), and is then held -- the platform's freeze (`HOLD = 'position'`) or the engine's NoMovement restriction (`'restriction'`, `Open77.character.setFrozen`); one that takes a standing body off the ground is let go and the other one is used for the rest of the session -- and it must stand, held, two looks more before the curtain comes down. It is never moved while held (a move -- a turn back, a new end point -- lets go first, and the body is held again once it stands again), and never under the curtain. A body that has not stood `GROUND_WAIT_MS` after it was put there is put 5 cm over the ground under it once and waited for as long again; then there is no clip, and the body is shown where it is. A turn of the body once the clip is asked is said, not turned back: a move then would take the body off the ground under the clip. Every change of what the body's game says of it is a line in the client log, with its height over the ground, its speed up or down and the screen (`exit body: on the ground, 0.00 m over the ground, 0.3s after the seat was left (not held, the image on)`), and every half second while it is off the ground.

**The door as each game draws it.** For the Excalibur the client reads where its own game draws the door: the door's slots on the hull (`AIRFRAMES` ... `DOOR_SLOTS`, `Open77.character.bonePosition`). Their place in the hull's frame says the door is where the plan has it, on the other side of the hull (the step is then planned on the hull turned round, and the server checks it the same way), or somewhere else (the numbers go in the log). The slots the hull publishes are listed once per record.

**The exit's drag.** The exit un-masks the hull on the exiting player's game, and for a fifth of a second its engine draws it back where its first flight on that game began -- the pad its never-moved chassis still holds -- and, on 2.50.0+op77.156, carries the leaving body along: the body is dropped beside the dragged hull, and the platform's re-mask (150 ms later) brings the hull back but not the body (XyberRP Staging, 2026-10-28: an Excalibur flown 14.2 m off its spawn pad, the body 13.3 m from it after the pilot's exit and 15.5 m after the back seat's -- past `NEAR_METRES`, so that exit was taken for a server's move, and had no clip). Since 1.6.1 a body within `NEAR_METRES` of the hull its game drew at the exit's first look, or within `PAD_REACH_METRES` of the pad that game's engine keeps for the hull (where it stood the first time that game simulated it with the body aboard, then each drag an exit sees -- for a drag that goes by between two frames), is an exit: the body is put beside the hull where it stands, as for any exit, and the guard puts it back if the platform drops it on the dragged hull again. The exiting game may also have sent the dragged pose as the hull's own (it simulated the hull; a first sample after a grant is allowed 300 m), and the server may take it -- on Staging it did, refused the hull's real pose after it, and the watch's heal then re-published the pad: the AV jumped 14 m back to where it was spawned. So the exiting game tells the server what its engine drew (`DRAG`), and the server puts an EMPTY hull it now has on that drag, within `DRAG_WINDOW_MS` of the exit, back where its own scans had it before the exit (`WATCH.DRAG_UNDO`, server/watch.lua) -- also on a viewer's word that it draws the hull there, and never heals onto the drag; meanwhile the exiting game does not plan on a network frame that carries it (`DRAG_HOLD_MS`).

**The hull the clip is planned on.** At the instant of an exit the engine may still draw the hull where its flight began (37 m, 7.2 m and 2.5 m measured), so the exit plans on where the network has the hull -- the pose the server holds and every other game draws -- and takes the engine's own frame only within `AGREE_METRES` (0.5 m) and 10 degrees of it. A vehicle snapshot's `entity` is 0 while its hull is not streamed, and the platform answers an entity of 0 with the local player's own geometry: no read here or in open77_avlanding passes it or takes such an answer for a hull. As soon as the hull's frame is steady after the exit (the engine's and the network's agreeing twice in a row, from 0.6 s), and again before the clip is asked, the walk-out is planned again on the hull as it stands; a body whose end point moved more than `REPLAN_METRES` is moved there with the image on (early, before the client's exit camera arms its own "first move" rule, `vflt-exit-tpp-off`, and with the ground under the gear measured again; a move made once third person is drawn waits for it to be drawn again and swings the camera again), and a door whose step hangs higher over the end point than the clip's first step can stand on (`STEP` of the airframe, `M.StepBand`) is waited for while the server may still set the hull down on its gear (the ground under the gear measured again), up to `SETTLE_MS` -- and then turned about the door or played within a relaxed reach (below); a step that never comes within reach plays no clip, and the body is shown standing beside the door instead of stepping out of the air. The ask carries the plan; the server holds it against its own hull (`PLAN_METRES`, the step, the body within `BODY_METRES`) and answers a plan made on a hull elsewhere with its own pose, which the client plans on once more. The ground measure under the gear is always sent: the server says why it did or did not lower the hull, including "already stands on its gear".

**The step on a slope.** An AV parked where the ground falls away beside its door leaves the door's step higher over the clip's end point than on a flat pad -- 0.92 m on XyberRP Staging (2026-10-28, 14:41:26: an Excalibur on its gear on a slope, from the back seat), past the 0.25 to 0.85 m the clip's first step can stand on, so that exit had no clip. Since 1.6.2 a step out of that band is first turned about the door: the clip still starts on the door, and its path swings `STEP_TURNS` (15 then 30 degrees, either way, `STEP_TURN_MAX`) along the hull to where the ground stands higher, the smallest turn that brings the step within the band winning. While the step is prepared (the image on) the client foresees the step the hull will have once it is on its gear, and moves the body to the turned end point then; a step out of reach only at the final look (under the curtain) lifts the curtain once for the move. When no turn does, a step within the relaxed band (`STEP_UNDER_RELAXED`, `STEP_OVER_RELAXED`: 0.15 to 1.00 m for the Excalibur's step) is played as it is -- its first frames a little off the step, under the fade-in; past that, no clip. The ask carries the turn (`stepTurn`); the server plans on its own hull turned the same and holds the step to the relaxed band. Every verdict is in the client's log, with the heights on this screen and on the network's hull.

**The step up into the door (1.7.0).** With open77_avexit 1.1.0 running, the key boards the body the way Rosalind Myers boards her AV in Phantom Liberty: it turns to the door and steps up into it (the clip `enter`; `enter_m` for a door on the right until 1.8.0, which boards such a door plainly: 2.70 to 5.30 s of her entry, 2.6 s, its first frame where the player entity stands, its last in the door 1.17 m ahead, 0.15 m to the right and 0.53 m up), and the seat comes at its end. The server opens the door on the key and asks the client for the step (`ENTER`); the client plans the clip's first spot with the exit's own plan (the door point, the clip's reach, `STEP_TURNS` on a slope, the door's step within the clip's reach of the spot's ground), puts the body there facing the door with the image on, lets it land and stand, turns it back when it faces more than `FACING_DEGREES` off, holds it, changes the view to third person (the stand-in, the only body the clip plays on on that screen) and asks (`ENTER_READY`). The server judges it again in full -- the verdict, the first spot against its own hull (`PLAN_METRES`), the body within `BODY_METRES` of it, the step within reach -- plays the clip for everybody no sooner than `DOOR_OPEN_MS` after the key, tells the client the seat's time (`ENTERING`), and seats the body `SEAT_EARLY_MS` (0.2 s) before the clip ends through the platform's own mount; the client lets go of the hold `SEAT_LEAD_MS` before that, and the view is the seat's own again the moment the seat is taken. Since 1.7.1 neither change of view is blacked out -- 1.7.0 put a short curtain over both, "two black screens" in four seconds for the owner (Staging, 2026-10-29), where the boarding's change of view takes a fifth of a second; `CURTAIN = true` and `SEAT_CURTAIN = true` bring them back. A seat the platform refuses as the clip ends is tried once more after the clip and its blend. A hull the flight parked over its gear -- a fresh Excalibur hangs 0.4 m over it, its door's step 1.07 m up, past the clip's reach: the owner's first boarding of one was plain -- is set down on its gear first (1.7.1): when every step the plan tries is too high, or only within the relaxed reach on a hull hanging over its gear, the client measures the ground under the gear feet and answers with it (`setDown`); the server lowers the empty hull onto it (server/land.lua, as after an exit; `SET_DOWN`) and asks for the step again `SET_DOWN_WAIT_MS` (1 s) later, and the client plans it on the hull as its game draws it, waiting `SET_DOWN_LOOK_MS` (2 s) at most for the hull to be drawn down there. Everything that fails -- no style, a client that does not know the step (an older client), no ground or a step out of reach, a body that will not stand or face the door, a hull that moved, no word within `ASK_MS` -- seats the body at once, as before 1.7.0, and both logs say why. Every boarding is one scene of the logger (below): every screen near it reports the step a moment into it and as it ends, and the server writes a verdict. The style is given when the player's client says hello (`ASSIGN = 'join'`), so the first boarding of a session plays the step too. `ENTER_ANIM = false` switches it off.

**A weapon drawn, and a game without the clips (1.7.2).** The platform plays no clip on a body with a weapon drawn: the owner's first boarding of 1.7.1 stood still at the door with one out, and was seated without a step (Staging, 2026-10-29). A weapon drawn at the key is put away first (`Open77.weapons.holster`, the loadout left as it is; `player.weapons.edit`), and the step waits `HOLSTER_WAIT_MS` (1.5 s) at most for the hands to be empty -- asking once more halfway -- before the boarding is plain (`HOLSTER = false` leaves the weapon). A game whose open77_avexit could not register the package plays nothing however the server asks: the server registers it from its own files, a game only from the archive it started with, and a game the launcher prepared with another world's mods has the wrong one (`locomotion_archive_missing_restart_required`). That evening every boarding held the body still at the door for three seconds and seated it, every exit stood it still, and the logs said "nothing wrong" -- they measured where the body stood, not whether a clip could be drawn. Since 1.7.2 the client asks open77_avexit (1.1.1: `exports.open77_avexit:Status()`, `AvDoor.AnimPackage`); a game that cannot draw the clips says so in its hello, with what its launcher prepared it for (`Open77.session.launcherContext`: the server id, the endpoint and the mods' digest, against the server's own "Required mods: ... digest=" line), tells its player once to restart the game through the Open77 launcher (`files.missing`), and boards and steps out plainly at once: no hold, no hide, no scene. The server log names it (`player 1's game has no AV animation package (locomotion_archive_missing_restart_required): ...; its launcher started it for server 00000000-... at 203.0.113.10:11840 with the mods of digest fd6aa98697a7 -- compare this server's "Required mods: ... digest=" line`). Two pictures more are taken just after the seat (`seated+0.15`, `seated+0.6`): the owner saw the screen go black as the body was seated with no curtain asked for.

**The stand-in first (1.7.3).** The clip of a player's own step plays, on that player's screen, on the platform's third-person stand-in body -- and a clip that starts while the stand-in is still being made or dressed is never drawn on it. The first third person of a game session makes the stand-in: on the owner's laptop (11 to 18 frames a second) 2.4 s from its spawn to dressed, with a 0.64 s hitch in it. 1.7.2 asked for third person only once the body was held, gave up waiting at 2.5 s and asked the step anyway: the diag pictures of that boarding (Staging, 2026-10-29) show the stand-in standing still at the door through the whole clip, then the cut into the seat ("i get blackscreen the teleport into the av"). Since 1.7.3 third person is asked at the key (`VIEW_EARLY`), after the body is put on its spot, so the stand-in is made while the body is placed, held and its weapon put away; the step is asked only once this screen draws the stand-in, for `VIEW_SETTLE_MS` (250 ms) or settled; `VIEW_WAIT_MS` (7 s) counts from the key, and a screen that has not drawn it by then boards plainly at once, said (`VIEW_REQUIRED`; false asks the step anyway, for the others, as 1.7.2 did). A third person this server or game never gives (`refused_by_policy`, no permission, no host support) boards plainly at the key, without holding the body. `ASK_MS` is 10 s (the server waits a second more). The client's lines say when third person was asked and drawn (`third person drawn 3.3s after the key ...`) and, as the clip begins, whether this screen draws the stand-in (`this screen draws the stand-in (settled, proxy)` or `does NOT draw the stand-in ...: the clip is not seen here`); pictures are taken as the step is asked (`asked`) and at the seat's own frame (`seated+0.0`).

**The game's own entry, shut (1.7.4).** An aircraft answers F twice: the door card (the step up, the seat it may take) and the game's own vehicle entry, which mounts the body in the seat whose door the eye is on, at once and without a step. Which one F reaches depends on where the player looks, so some boardings had no step. The platform removes every mount choice from a vehicle that is locked for the player looking at it, and a lock can be one player's alone (`Open77.vehicles.setLockedForPlayer`). While a player stands on foot within `NATIVE_ENTRY.METRES` (12 m) of an aircraft, the server locks it for that player only; past `RELEASE_METRES` (16 m), once seated, when they leave, when the resource stops, or just before the card seats them, they get back exactly what they had (their own exception, or none). The canonical lock, the one vehicle keys turn, is never touched, and who may board is still decided on it. While a hold stands, that player's game reads the aircraft as locked (a keys row shows it so); the key still turns the real lock. Every hold and release is a server log line, and a boarding that still comes through the game's own entry is a warning with the reason. `/avdoor.status` counts them. `NATIVE_ENTRY.SHUT = false` leaves the game's own entry open.

**The stand-in made ahead (1.7.5).** The step up plays on the platform's third-person stand-in, and the first third person of a game session has to make that body: spawned, dressed, its equipment worn. The platform gives that 3 s; past it, it shows first person again and tries once more 3 s later. On a game running at 4 to 18 frames a second, the first boarding of a session therefore stood still at the door for about 7 s and was then seated plainly (1.7.4 gave up after 7 s). Once made, the stand-in stays for the session, parked and hidden. So the client now makes it ahead, out of sight: `STAND_IN.AFTER_MS` (5 s) after the server has heard the client, or as soon as a door card first comes up, it asks for third person and lets the request go on the frame the platform reports that it is building the body behind the first-person view (`holding_current`). The platform keeps first person on screen while it builds, a request let go at that point changes nothing on screen, and the body is kept for the next third person, so the first boarding draws third person within a second or two. Nothing is done when the stand-in was already drawn this session, while the body is seated, down, boarding or stepping out, or while another resource or a world state holds the view. A boarding or an exit that starts during a try takes the view over without the try releasing it. Every try is logged in the client log with each answer the platform gave (`stand-in: ...`), and a client that shows third person after the release is reported once and not tried again. The boarding itself now waits up to 12 s for the stand-in (`VIEW_WAIT_MS`; `ASK_MS` 15 s), long enough for the platform's own retry, and logs when the platform's 3 s ran out. A boarding seated before its own screen's last sample of the step (a slow game) is reported as seated, not as a fault.

**The style before the stand-in (1.7.6).** The exit style (`EXIT_ANIM.STYLE`, open77_avexit's package) is given when a player is ready, and the platform gives a style only to a ready, living player. A player whose hello came before they were ready, or who joined dead, was refused (`player_unavailable`) and not asked again until the first boarding's key; and giving a style rebuilds the body every game draws, the player's own third-person stand-in included, so that first boarding waited for a new stand-in (Staging: 8.6 s from the key). The server now asks again every `STYLE_RETRY_MS` (2 s) for up to `STYLE_RETRY_MAX_MS` (5 min) after such a refusal, with a line when it is given; the client makes the stand-in ahead only once it has the style, and makes it again if a style comes after it was made. A style another resource holds, or a server without the package, means no step and nothing made ahead.

**Into the seat (1.7.7).** The door's clip ends standing in the door and the seat was a cut, so the body was never seen sitting down. A seat whose slot is known (`AIRFRAMES` ... `SEATS` ... `SIT`; the Rayfield Excalibur's pilot seat, `seat_front_left`) is now boarded with `ENTER_ANIM.SIT_CLIP` (`sit`, open77_avexit 1.2.0): the game's own boarding of that AV in the side quest sq012, which walks up to the door, steps into the cabin, turns and sits down (5.6 s). Its plan is made from the seat rather than the door (`AvDoor.SitPlan`): the body is put where the clip's end lands on the seat's slot, facing the slot's way -- the Excalibur's front seats face the tail -- so the clip ends where the seat holds the body, and the seat comes `SEAT_EARLY_MS` before the clip's end as before. **Every screen in step.** Each game draws an exit on its own, and every game but the exiting player's first runs the platform's own dismount for that body, which holds it in its seat for up to 2.5 s (2.8 s measured on the laptop). A step started 2.2 s after the exit began there before the body was out of its seat. So the exit is one scene (`server/sync.lua`, `client/sync.lua`): every other player within `SYNC.RANGE_METRES` (150 m) is told of it; each screen whose client said hello (it says hello until the server answers -- a hello sent while the game loads goes nowhere -- and a word from one that never did counts as its hello) is waited for. Each watches its own copy of the body (`Open77.players.entity`, `Open77.character.state`: its vehicle mount, its exit workspot) and says when it is out of its seat. The step waits for all of them, under the exiting player's curtain, at most `HOLD_MAX_MS` (3.5 s) after the ask and never past `WAIT_MAX_MS` (5 s) after the exit. A screen still holding the body after `READY_MAX_MS` says so and is no longer waited for, and one that does not draw the body says so at once. A screen that draws the AV more than `HULL_METRES` from the server's pose gets that pose published again, once per exit and only for an empty hull nobody poses. `SYNC = false` starts the step when the exiting game asks.

**The logger.** Every screen that drew the exit reports what it drew `SAMPLE_MS` (0.2 s) into the step and again as it ends, the exiting player's own screen included: whether the body was in its seat, how far it stood from the step's end point (`BODY_METRES`), the heading it drew the body at against the step's (`FACING_DEGREES`), where the step's first frame stood -- from that place and that heading -- against the door's step on the AV as that screen draws it (`STEP_METRES`), that door against the server's and its door slot, whether the body was on the ground, had a weapon drawn or was drawn from stale movement (`STALE_MS`; the platform plays no clip in any of those), another player's hips (on its feet's point a moment into the step: the step was not drawn on that screen; a screen waits up to a second for its copy of the clip before it reports), where the AV stood against the server's pose, the view (third person on the stand-in, the camera in front) and the frame rate. The server writes one line per report, a warning ending `-- WRONG: ...` for anything off, and closes each exit with a verdict, `exit N of player P: verdict -- ...: nothing wrong` or the list of what went wrong. Each game also measures its own frame rate (three frames a second) and the cost of this resource's watch pass. Under 15 frames a second, or past 8 ms for a pass, it logs a `client health:` warning once a minute; otherwise it logs an all-clear every five minutes.

**The ground test.** `/avdoor.groundtest` in chat runs, on foot and on the typing player's game alone, fourteen steps on the standing body -- standing still, moved where it stands, held by the platform's freeze, let go, moved while held, let go, held again, moved 5 cm up, held by the NoMovement restriction, the screen black (a native fade), moved 5 cm up under the black, the image back -- reading every frame what its game says of the ground and of the screen, and writes each change and each step's end to that client's log (`ground test 13/14 (moved 5 cm up where it stands, under the black): at its end off the ground ...`, then `ground test over: 1 on the ground; 2 ...`). About twenty-five seconds, the screen black for about 3.5 s of them; every hold and the fade are let go at the end.

**What the screen showed.** During each exit a game plays or draws, it takes a few small pictures of its own frame (`Open77.screen.capture`: the game's back buffer alone, never the desktop or Open77's overlays) -- a frame after the exit, the body standing beside the door as the curtain comes down, the image back, and through the step to its end -- and keeps them on that machine in this resource's client store (`Open77.kvp`, under `red4ext/plugins/Open77/storage/kvp` in the game's folder) for the last `DIAG.KEEP` (2) exits, each with what the client knew as it was taken (the body's place, heading and ground, the hold, the view, the clip's time). They are written after the exit, never during it: every call into the store reads the whole file and writes it again on the game's own thread (`scripting/src/KvpStore.cpp`), a hitch in the step it pictures. So they are held in memory and written once no exit runs on that screen and none has been taken for 2.5 s, one store call a resume, a quarter-second apart -- about ten seconds after the step ends. Nothing is sent anywhere. [`scripts/avdoor/read_avdoor_shots.py`](../../../scripts/avdoor/read_avdoor_shots.py) writes them out as JPEG files. `DIAG.SHOTS = false` takes none.

**Within the game's budget.** The host kills a resume of a resource's Lua at its 10 000th instruction when its slice of the frame is spent -- on a loaded client always (`scripting/src/ResourceHost.cpp`, `InstructionHook`) -- and the 1.5.0 laptop lost two passes of an exit to it mid-way. The settings are read once (`M.SettingsChanged()` reads them again), the client's watch is four jobs (the seat and the exit, the card, the sight check, the exit scene), each its own resume, and an exit's heavy steps -- the hull's frames and the ground, the plan, the door's slots -- each take a resume of their own (`M.Breathe`). The offline suite runs every resume under the same rule (`tests/world.lua`, `W.budget`), and every script in its host's sandbox: a client has no `setmetatable`, `getmetatable`, `load`, `os`, `io`, `debug` or `package` (1.5.1 called `setmetatable` and no client could load it). Since 1.7.0 every pass of every job is timed on the host's clock, and a minute after the start, then every five minutes, each side writes what each of its jobs cost: `job costs over the last 300s (open77_avdoor): scan 600 pass(es), 0.21 ms on average, 1.80 ms the dearest (42s ago); snap ...; tend ...` -- passes, mean, dearest and when, how many took 50 ms or more, any that failed (`M.COST_FIRST_MS`, `M.COST_EVERY_MS`, `M.COST_SLOW_MS`). Since 1.7.1 a pass's own cost leaves out the frames it waited in its breaths (the longest pass with its waits is said beside it: 1.7.0's dearest landing-sign look, 532 ms at four frames a second, was two breaths), and the line ends with the dearest natives the passes called (`travel.teleport`, `character.bonePosition`, `screen.capture`, `world.entityGeometry`, `perspective.setThirdPerson`, the holds: `M.Native`); a native call of 20 ms or more is a line at once, with the pass it ran in (`slow native: travel.teleport took 41.2 ms in one call (the watch pass; ...)`, `M.NATIVE_SLOW_MS`, once a minute a native) -- an exit's pass on the laptop took 64.5 ms of Lua in one frame, and a boarding's 44.5 ms. The server's clock stands still within a tick: its line counts the passes and says so. The hull's whole slot list (70 slots on the Excalibur), read once a record at its first exit since 1.5.0, is read only when asked (`EXIT_ANIM.LIST_SLOTS = true`): the door's own slot is read without it.

**The watch.** Each client compares where it draws nearby aircraft it does not simulate with the newest sample of them, and tells the server when the two drift apart; the server logs it beside its own reads, logs every change of an aircraft's physics owner, and re-publishes an EMPTY, unfrozen hull a client draws far from its canonical pose (at most once per 10 s per hull, never with anyone aboard) -- unless that pose is an exit's drag, which it puts back where the hull stood instead (see **The exit's drag**). `WATCH = false` switches it off.

**The landing call.** A MaxTac or Trauma Team AV that a player flies says "Warning. Clear the area. MaxTac landing." (or "Trauma Team landing.") on its loudspeaker while it comes down, from its pilot's reported descent until it touches down, faded out when it lands. Each game plays the line itself in its own voice-over language: English, French, German, Spanish, Italian, Polish, Portuguese, Russian, Ukrainian, Czech, Hungarian, Chinese, Korean or Japanese (`audio/`, the clips the XyberRP servers play). `CALLOUTS = false` switches it off.

Every number is in `shared/config.lua`, each one with what was measured to choose it.

```lua
exports.open77_avdoor:forget(vehicleId)

exports.open77_avdoor:unpad(vehicleId)

-- landingSite asks the players' games for the ground, so it WAITS: call it through Open77.exports.call.
CreateThread(function()
    local site, why = Open77.exports.call("open77_avdoor", "landingSite", { x = x, y = y, z = z },
        { bearing = 90, near = 10 }):await()
end)
```

A rule, a landing call and a pad are taken away when the resource that put them up stops. The server events `open77_avdoor:onBoarded(playerId, vehicleId, seat, role)` and `open77_avdoor:onExited(playerId, vehicleId, seat, how)` report each boarding through the door and each seat of an aircraft left.

Console: `avdoor.why` (any player: why the nearest aircraft does or does not open its door to you), `avdoor.ride` (any player, 1.8.0: `on` boards them as a passenger from then on, `off` as before, no word switches), `avdoor.status` (restricted: doors open, rules, players at a door, hulls settling, the watch, pads and landing calls, exit clips, and the exit sync: scenes, holds, late screens, reports, what was wrong, re-publishes), `avdoor.groundtest` (any player, on foot: the ground test above, on their own game).

### Logs

Every step is a line in the server or client log: each card shown, each press and its verdict, each door opened and closed, each exit (where the body was put, which airframe's door, how often the platform moved it, when the curtain came down and up, and why), each exit clip (the style given or refused, the plan, the ask, the answer, the hide and show, the freeze and the view), each hull set down on its gear or why it was left where it was (the ground under each foot, as the exiting game measured it; a glide's start, its end and why one stopped), each sound and which listener played or refused it, each landing call and pad, each drift a client saw and each heal.

Each exit is a scene in the server log, from `exit N of player P (seat of hull): ... other screen(s) draw it` to its verdict. Each client says hello until answered (`the server heard this client's hello`). On the exiting client, `exit from ... seen at its first frame`, every `exit body: ...` (on the ground or not, mounted, in a workspot; held or not), `the body stands on the ground`, `held, and still on the ground`, the door slot (`the AV's door as this game draws it -- slot ...`), every `the body turned N deg, from A to B (...)` with what was going on, each `turned back (n of 3 ...)`, and `the body faces ... as the step begins`. In between come each screen's word (`drew player P out of the seat 2.9s after the exit`), the hold (`held 700 ms for the other screens`), any re-publish, and two reports from every screen. A warning marks everything wrong. To find what went wrong, search the server log for `WRONG` and `verdict`. Each client log carries the same lines from its own side, plus its `client health:` line.

The exit key (1.9.0): the client logs `exit key open77_avdoor_leave (F) taken for <seat> of <hull>` as a body sits in a Manticore and `... dropped ... the game's own again` once it is out (`could not be taken (<reason>)` where the game cannot consume the key), `exit key F pressed`, and at the exit `asked through the exit key` -- or `the game's own exit, animated -- not asked through the exit key and the engine still mounts the body` when the game stepped it out itself. The server logs `exit key of player P: stepped out of <seat> of aircraft <hull> ... through the forced exit`, or `refused (<reason>)`: `not_seated`, `not_this_hull`, `already_leaving`, `not_an_animated_exit`, `dead`, `busy`, `forced_exit_refused`.

### Ported from XyberRP

This is the `avdoor` module of XyberRP's `opx_infinity`, which ran on the XyberRP servers. Do not run both on one server: every aircraft would answer two doors. The resource warns in the log when it finds `opx_infinity` running (on a server that keeps opx_infinity, set `OPX.Config.MODULES.avdoor.enabled = false` there). Its autopilot companion is [open77_avdrive](../open77_avdrive/); the cursive "Open77" under a landing aircraft is [open77_avlanding](../open77_avlanding/).
