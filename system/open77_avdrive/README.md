# open77_avdrive

This resource is an autopilot for AVs (the flying vehicles): whoever flies the aircraft drops a waypoint on the map and presses F10; the screens of everybody aboard fade to black, the aircraft is flown to the pin under the black, and the screens come back with the pilot holding the controls in a hover over the pin, to land. F10 again takes the controls back at any moment. (`TRAVEL = 'fly'` flies it there instead, in three legs, the way 1.0.0 did.)

- **Docs:** [Network vehicles](https://open2077.net/docs/vehicles)
- **Starts:** on its own
- **Needs:** nothing
- **Runs on:** server and client

## Use it

List `open77_avdrive` in `resources.load`. It works in any aircraft the Open77 client flies as an AV (records `Vehicle.av_*` and `Vehicle.max_tac_av`), for whoever flies it: the player in the pilot seat; else the one whose game simulates the aircraft (it holds the physics lease, or the platform names it the controller); else the only one aboard. The Open77 client flies an AV from whichever seat its crew boarded through, so F10 works from the back seat too; a passenger behind somebody who flies is refused by name. The key is rebindable from Pause > Settings > Key bindings ("Toggle the AV autopilot").

**The arrival (the default; flown under the black since 1.2.0).** F10 fades the screens of everybody aboard to black (600 ms). Once the pilot's game says its screen is black, the server freezes the hull and FLIES it to 8 m over the pin with `Open77.vehicles.setTransform` every 100 ms: up to 60 m over the higher of where it is and the pin (60 m/s), across at 150 m/s, down to the hover, facing the way it goes -- never a step longer than one tick of that flight (15 m), about 10 s for a kilometre. A long way is flown faster, so the flying never lasts more than 30 s. The pilot's game says four times a second where it has the aircraft -- its body in it, the engine's own truth, and its copy of the hull -- and whether the game's own loading screen is up (`Open77.screen.loadingState`, on a client that has it). The flight HOLDS while that body is more than 200 m behind the aircraft the server flies (8 s at most), while a loading screen is up or the game has gone silent (30 s at most); a flight that never goes on is given up: the aircraft is put where the pilot's game has it, and the controls and the screens come back with "Autopilot off: the aircraft could not be flown there". Over the pin, the pilot's game lets the world there stream in (1.2 s) and measures the ground under the pin: a ray straight down every 250 ms until two agree (6 s at most), meeting the static meshes, and the open terrain on a client whose rays take collision groups. When that ground is higher than the pin's own height (a roof), the hover is raised to 8 m over it, in steps too; it is never lowered. Then the freeze comes off, and 400 ms later the screens come back (800 ms). The pilot's game then watches for the controls -- its game simulating the aircraft again -- for 5 s, and says whether it got them; when it did not, the server asks the platform to hand the aircraft to the pilot (`Open77.vehicles.requestAuthority`), the one grant that is not a steal, and logs the answer. Nobody aboard sees a move. A game that cannot fade is flown in view, and the log says so. A pilot whose game never says anything is never flown, and is handed back where it stood. Whatever ends an arrival, every screen comes back. The fade's own safety deadline (60 s) brings the image back even if the server never does.

**The flight (`TRAVEL = 'fly'`).** Three legs: a climb at 5 m/s to 120 m above the higher of the take-off point and the waypoint, a cruise at 22 m/s (the manual flight's own ceilings), and a descent at 3.5 m/s to 8 m above the pin. The server poses the hull every 100 ms with `Open77.vehicles.setTransform`, and the hull is frozen for the whole run, so the pilot's own client cannot fight the flight. The freeze comes off when the run ends and the aircraft is the pilot's again. Every pose makes the pilot's view flicker (see below), so this is for a server that wants the flight seen from outside.

Either way it ends in a hover rather than a landing. The pilot lands.

A run also ends, by name, when the pilot leaves the seat or disconnects, the aircraft is removed, the run passes 20 minutes, or the resource stops; nobody is left frozen in mid-air or looking at a black screen. Refusals say which check failed: not seated, not the one who flies it, not an AV, no waypoint, the map could not be read, the waypoint is more than 4 km away, or the aircraft is already under autopilot.

Every number is in `shared/config.lua`.

Server exports: `exports.open77_avdrive:status()` (the flights in progress) and `exports.open77_avdrive:cancel(playerId, why)`. Console: `avdrive.status` (restricted).

Every engage, refusal, leg and end is a line in the server log, with the hull, the route, the legs' planned and flown times, the poses sent and any the host refused. An arrival adds who is aboard, when the pilot's screen went black, the route under the black and each leg done, every 2 s how far it has flown and how far behind the pilot's body is, every hold and why (behind, loading, silent) and how long, a flight given up and where the aircraft was put back, the ground the pilot's game measured (how, how many reads, how long) and where that game had the aircraft then, any raise, the engine flag and the platform's lease at the hand-over, whether the pilot's game took the aircraft back, any ask to the platform and its answer, and what ended it. Each game aboard logs its own fade, and at the move, every 2 s of the flight, when the image comes back and 1 s and 3 s later, where it draws the aircraft and the body in it, whether it simulates the aircraft, whether the aircraft is frozen, whether a loading screen is up and what the engine's own fade is doing; and it logs every loading screen of the game that opens or closes.

### Measured in game

On a loopback server, 2026-10-24, in a Rayfield Excalibur boarded through the door card: F10 without a waypoint was refused by name (`noWaypoint`); with one 198 m away the run flew its plan (climb 24.0 s, cruise 9.1 s, descent 32.4 s for 24.0, 9.0 and 32.3 planned), sent 549 poses with none refused, ended "arrived" in the hover and gave the controls back. Two things a pilot sees:

- **The run moves in steps.** Every pose is a `setTransform`, and the pilot's screen shows each one as a step: ten a second, up to 1 m in the climb and descent and 4 m at cruise speed. Manual flight has none. `TICK_MS` trades step size for packets; smooth server-driven flight needs motion the platform interpolates, which `setTransform` is not.
- **The pilot's client keeps claiming the hull.** Each pose revokes the physics lease and the seated pilot's client claims it again (about 1,100 authority changes in the 65 s run, each a line from `open77_vehicles`). The freeze discards that client's transform reports, so it cannot move the hull, but the churn is real.

A pilot who boarded through the game's own entry may sit in another seat and fly from it (in game, the Excalibur's own entry seated one in `seat_back_left`, and so does open77_avdoor's `avdoor.ride`). 1.0.0, like the opx_infinity module it was ported from, served `seat_front_left` only and answered any other seat with `notPilot`: on Staging (2026-10-30) opx_infinity's module said "Only the pilot's seat has the autopilot." to a player flying an Excalibur from `seat_back_left`. 1.1.0 serves whoever flies the aircraft, from any seat.

### Why it is flown under the black and not placed (1.2.0)

1.1.0 placed the aircraft over the pin in ONE move under the black. On Staging (2026-10-30, 17:15) that was a 1,080 m move of an Excalibur with its pilot in the back seat: the pilot's game drew it over the pin by the time the image came back, but no longer simulated it ("lease of player 0" 1 s and 3 s later), and the player was "stuck on a loadscreen forever". The platform revoked the pilot's lease twice, 0.2 s apart (epochs 9 and 11, both `serverrevoke`): the first is the move itself; the second, by our reading of the platform's code, is its revoke of an owner that reports itself not ready -- and a game that is not ready may not claim the aircraft back. The platform's own teleport refuses a player in a vehicle ("there is no way to carry the car along"), and a long-distance teleport may open the game's loading screen. So 1.2.0 never moves the aircraft farther than one step of a fast flight, and lets the pilot's game set the pace. (What kept that game not ready is inferred: its log has no line about a loading screen, because 1.1.0 did not ask. 1.2.0 asks.)

### Why it arrives rather than flies (1.1.0)

`Open77.vehicles.setTransform` always revokes the physics lease, and the platform counts every occupant of an AV as its controller, so the pilot's game claims the aircraft straight back. Its chase camera lets go when it loses the aircraft and comes back when it has it again, so under a flight posed ten times a second the pilot's view flickers between the chase camera and first person for the whole flight. open77_avdoor's pad snap flew an occupied AV the same way, and the pilot could not see the aircraft land: 129 poses in 9.8 s, the hull changing hands 252 times in 10 s, and the pilot's game letting its chase camera go 116 times in one glide and 122 in the next (Staging, 2026-10-30). No platform call moves an AV without revoking its lease, and a resource cannot hold the camera of a player in a vehicle. So by default the move happens under a fade to black.

### Ported from XyberRP

This is the `avdrive` module of XyberRP's `opx_infinity`, which ran on the XyberRP servers. Do not run both on one server: with opx_infinity running, switch its avdrive module off (`OPX.Config.MODULES.avdrive.enabled = false` in its `config/avdrive.lua`), or one key press may start two autopilots on one aircraft. This resource warns in the log when opx_infinity runs beside it. Its companion, [open77_avdoor](../open77_avdoor/), is how a player gets into the pilot's seat of a parked AV.
