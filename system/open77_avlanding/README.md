# open77_avlanding

"WARNING" in bold block letters, flashing red on the ground under an aircraft that comes down to it, inside a ring of warning words -- "WARNING • AV LANDING ZONE • KEEP CLEAR •" -- that turns slowly round it: the landing sign (1.4.0). 1.0 to 1.3 flashed a cursive "Open77" in a plain ring, and the Open77 client's dot-matrix OPEN77 of sixty red mine-laser beams came before that -- a dot matrix of beams does not read as letters.

- **Starts:** on its own
- **Needs:** `requiredMods.enabled = true` (the sign is a preload archive: the launcher installs it before the game starts)
- **Runs on:** client (the sign); the server only answers `/avlanding.test`

## What you see

Under any aircraft (the records the Open77 client flies as AVs, the MaxTac and Trauma Team airframes), the moment its lowest point comes within 6 m of the ground on its way down, the sign pops onto the ground under the hull, in the landing lasers' red: a ring about 7.6 m across, lit the whole time; inside it a band of words, "WARNING • AV LANDING ZONE • KEEP CLEAR •" in half-metre capitals round the circle, tops outward, with a thin line inside the band; and in the centre "WARNING", about 4.2 m long and 0.6 m tall, flashing -- half a second on, half a second off, the way the game's own STAY AWAY sign does. The word is turned so that the pilot behind the aircraft reads it; the ring with its band of words turns under it, 20 degrees a second, counterclockwise seen from above, so the words run past like a ticker (`RING_DEGREES_PER_SECOND`; this client turns it every `RING_MS`, 33 ms: the ring is an effect of its own). It follows the hull, stays while the aircraft stands on the ground in use (someone aboard -- the player themself, sitting in it, included -- its engine, a physics owner), and goes when it climbs away, or 8 s after a parked aircraft fell idle. Every game draws it under the aircraft it draws, at most the six nearest within 120 m. (`Open77.vehicles.nearby` counts who is aboard and says nothing of the engine: the client reads the rest from `Open77.vehicles.get` with each listing.)

The Open77 client draws its own landing lasers under the aircraft it flies (a ring of twelve red beams, an orbiting one, and on published clients sixty more in a dot-matrix OPEN77), all with one effect, `laser.mine` (`base\fx\devices\mine_laser\mine_laser_red.effect`). No Lua reaches them, so this resource's second archive (`dist/Open77AvLasersOff_<rev>.zip`) carries that effect drawn as nothing: alive and looping -- the client moves its beams every frame and would spawn an effect that ended again -- with its alpha, emission and size at zero. The sign is then the only marker under an AV. The archive is one of this server's world mods: the launcher lays it on Connect and takes it off when the player leaves, so while the player is here the game's own laser mines show no red beam either, and single player is untouched. The client source in this branch draws none of the old lasers at all (`kDrawLandingBeams`, `kDrawDotWordmark` in `client/src/api/Vehicles.cpp`), with the next client release.

It lies on the ground, not in it: on a slope, a road's camber or a kerb a flat sign laid at the height of the ground under its centre would sink in, so the client measures the ground under the whole sign -- 13 rays straight down: its centre, eight points on the ring's edge, four on the word's -- and lays the sign on the plane of that ground, tilted with it (20 degrees at most; steeper ground holds it at 20 and raises it over every sample) and raised until no sample is above it, 15 cm up. What is not that ground is left out: the street under a roof's edge, the top of a wall beside the pad (too far from the centre's height), a bench or a bollard (off the plane the other samples lie on). The ground is the static meshes and, on a client whose rays take collision groups (Stable 156 and later), the open terrain of the Badlands; an older client's rays meet no ground over terrain, and draws no sign there.

The plane is the dearest thing the resource does, and the game runs a resource's Lua in slices with an instruction budget (about 60 000 instructions): one plane tries at most `FIT_TRIPLES` (96) of the planes through three of its samples (the centre's first; a plane every sample lies on ends it at once), about 22 000 instructions over rough ground, and one look over the aircraft measures at most `FITS_PER_LOOK` (1) plane -- another aircraft's waits for the next look, a twentieth of a second. Since 1.3.2 the work is also cut into resumes the host never kills: a resume that reaches 10 000 instructions is killed once its slice of the frame is spent -- on a loaded client, about 0.3 ms (`scripting/src/ResourceHost.cpp`, `InstructionHook`) -- so the triples are tried a dozen at a time, the rays and the fit in resumes of their own, and one aircraft is looked at per resume (open77_avdoor's `tests/world.lua` runs this suite under that rule).

A GAME WITHOUT THIS ARCHIVE (1.4.2) -- one started with another world's mods, or without the launcher -- draws the game's own AV landing-area hologram instead (`base\fx\vehicles\av\_landing_area\v_av_landing_area_hologram.effect`, the effect the sign was cut from, in every copy of the game; `FALLBACK_EFFECT`), where and as the sign would lie, without the ring: the old lasers are drawn as nothing on this server, and a game started with another copy of Staging's mods had no landing marker at all (Staging, 2026-10-29). One line says so -- the archive is not in the game it started with, restart it through the Open77 launcher -- and every "on" line names the effect (`the game's own landing hologram (no sign archive in this game)`); `L.Status().fallback` is true. `FALLBACK_EFFECT = false`: nothing then.

An aircraft this game does not draw yet has no sign: its snapshot's `entity` is 0 until it is streamed, and the platform answers a geometry read of entity 0 with the local player's own body -- read that way, the sign of a hull that had just appeared 23 m away was laid under the player's feet (XyberRP Staging, 2026-10-28). An entity of 0 is never passed, and an answer for it is never taken for a hull.

Every number is in `shared/config.lua`. `AHEAD_METRES` moves the sign along the hull's own axis (0: straight under the hull's centre; 7: ahead of the nose, where a pilot's camera behind a hull that is touching down still sees the ground). `FIT_RADIUS_METRES = 0` and `FIT_INNER_METRES = 0` lay it flat on the ground under its centre, as 1.0 did.

## See it without an aircraft

`/avlanding.test`, typed in chat, lays the sign on the ground 8 m in front of you for 20 s, its top pointing away from you: whether this game has the archive, how it looks, which way it reads, how it lies on a slope. It is a server command (a server's chat hands a /command to the server, XyberRP's among them), which asks the caller's game to lay the sign, at most once every 3 s per player; the same name is a command of the game's own console too. The client log says where the sign went and how it lies, or why it did not (`avlanding.test (asked by the server): no sign: ...`); the server log names who asked.

## What it is made of

Two archives. The second is the old lasers' effect drawn as nothing (above; `scripts/avlanding/make_laser_off.py`). The first holds two effects, `open77\fx\avlanding\10c786843d78\open77_warning.effect` and `...\open77_warning_ring.effect`, in `dist/Open77AvLanding_10c786843d78.zip`:

- the game's own AV landing-area hologram (`base\fx\vehicles\av\_landing_area\v_av_landing_area_hologram.effect`) reduced to its signs' particle track and loop marker, its "away2" sign emitter taken once in each effect, drawn by the game's hologram particle shader, simulated in the effect's own space (so it turns and tilts with it):
  - the word on the flat "STAY AWAY" quad (`fx_av_landing_area_staty_away_sign.mesh`, 1.28 x 0.25 m) at 3.8 times its size, one particle a second living 0.5 s -- the flash -- 6 cm over the effect's origin;
  - the ring on the square stop-sign quad (`fx_av_landing_area_stop_sign.mesh`, 0.64 x 0.64 m) at 12.6 times its size, one particle a second living 1.05 s -- lit the whole time -- 4 cm over it;
- one texture for both, ours: "WARNING" in [Inter](https://rsms.me/inter/) Black, and the ring (3.44 to 3.79 m), the band of words in Inter ExtraBold (2.55 to 3.25 m) and the line (2.40 to 2.46 m), white on black in the two quads' corners of a 2048 x 2048 BC1 texture (`open77_warning_d.xbm`; Inter is under the SIL Open Font License 1.1);
- the landing lasers' red (238, 0, 50) at 20 times.

The recipe (the texture, the effect and particle system from the game's own, the archive and the zip, reproducible byte for byte) is in [`scripts/avlanding`](../../../scripts/avlanding/README.md).

## Logs

The ring's turn costs one effect update a sign every 33 ms; once a minute while rings turn the client says how many turns that was and what a pass cost (`landing sign rings: 1820 turn(s) in 1820 pass(es) over the last 60s, 0.041 ms a pass on average, 0.310 ms the dearest, 0 refused`). A ring the game refuses leaves the word drawn alone, in one line (`the landing sign's ring ... was refused (...): the word is drawn alone`).

Every pass of the client's three jobs -- the list, the look (the dearest: the ground's plane) and the ring's turn -- is timed on the host's clock; a minute after the start, then every five minutes, one line says what each cost, the same line as open77_avdoor's: `job costs over the last 300s (open77_avlanding): list 600 pass(es), 0.05 ms on average, 0.40 ms the dearest (12s ago); look 6000 pass(es), ...; ring 9091 pass(es), ...` -- with how many took 50 ms or more and how many FAILED (`L.COST_FIRST_MS`, `L.COST_EVERY_MS`, `L.COST_SLOW_MS` in `client/main.lua`). Since 1.4.1 a pass's own cost leaves out the frames it waited in its breaths (1.4.0's dearest look, 532 ms on a game at four frames a second, was two breaths; the longest with its waits is said beside it), and the line ends with the dearest natives: the rays, the hull's geometry, the effects' plays and updates are timed (`L.Native`), and one call of 20 ms or more is a line at once with the pass it ran in (`slow native: world.raycast took 23.0 ms in one call (the landing sign's look pass; ...)`, `L.NATIVE_SLOW_MS`). A pass that raises is said once (`the landing sign's look pass failed: ...`) and the job goes on.

Each aircraft taken in is a line (`watching <id> (<record>) for the landing sign: 30 m away, 0 aboard`), and so is a record that is not an aircraft (once) and why a watched aircraft has no sign, whenever that changes (`no landing sign under <id> (<record>): 0.0 m over the ground, nobody aboard, engine off, idle 12s ...`, `... in use (1 aboard): neither coming down ... nor within 1.5 m`, `... 14.2 m over the ground (shown within 6.0 m)`, `... no ground found under it`). Every sign on and off is a line in the client log, with the hull, its record, its clearance and speed, the place, the heading and the ground's plane (`ground plane 5.7 deg slope, falling toward heading 90 deg, 13 of 13 rays on the ground, 13 kept, raised 0.00 m`); a plane that changes under a sign is a line too (one every 5 s at most). The first ray says which ground this client's rays meet (`the ground under a sign: static meshes and terrain ...` or `... static meshes only, ...: no sign over open terrain`). A game without the archive says so once (`... is not in this game: install open77_avlanding's archive`).
