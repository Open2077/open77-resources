# open77_pedestrians

This resource brings Night City's sidewalks to life with server-owned NPCs: people sitting, leaning, praying or working at the game's own spots near each player, plus a walking crowd, all seen the same way by every player.

- **Docs:** [Server-owned NPCs](https://open2077.net/docs/npcs)
- **Starts:** on its own
- **Needs:** nothing
- **Runs on:** server and client

`open77_pedestrians` places server-owned static NPCs at authored community workspots near ready, living players: people praying, meditating, leaning on bars, sitting at tables. Every replica plays the same anchored workspot profile and clip. It starts automatically when loaded in `server.jsonc`.

The catalog (40,716 spots, 15,447 community entries, 318 NPC records, Phantom Liberty included) keeps community spots (regular and streamable community areas alike) whose NPC record is a peaceful crowd member, civilian or vendor (animation only, no shop): reaction preset `Civilian_*`, `NoReaction` or `Sleep_Civilian`, civilian, unaffiliated, Aldecaldos or Arasaka faction. Quest content stays out: records with a quest affiliation, templates under `quest\`, `media\`, `street_stories\` or `mini_world_stories\` (named characters such as Judy or Takemura), and records or community entries named after a quest, a scripted scene or a crime scene (`CorpoMan_q112`, `q307_alley_extra_02`, `mq306_*`, `sts_*`, `wst_*`, `ma_*_victim_*`, hostages, executions, cyberpsycho scenes). Children, robots, gangs, guards (Dogtown's Barghest citizens and the stadium market's guards and named staff included), police, Joytoy service records and parked vehicles are excluded, as are workspots in a scene folder wherever it sits (`quest`, `main_quests`, `minor_quests`, `side_quests`, `main_characters`, `synced`, `patrolling`, `street_stories`, `mini_world_stories`, `dynamic_events`, `encounter`), corpse poses (`dead`, `unconscious`, `corpse`) and threat poses (`afraid`, `hands_up`, `arrested`, `surrender`, `scared`, `handcuffs`). The looping work clips of a workspot are played, motion clips and those that hold an object included: the workspot spawns its own props (cigarettes, phones, tablets, food, guitars). Two kinds are left out: clips of a reaction sequence (bumps, the hands-up a threat triggers), and clips behind a required actor tag (`chubby`, `HelmetFull`, `Mask`…): a `chubby` clip played on an untagged NPC made the workspot exit and re-enter, the NPC standing up, walking off and sitting down again in a loop; the other tags are left out on the same grounds, untested. A workspot whose work only pauses on its idle plays that idle, and an idle-less `…__idle_loop__…` clip loops on itself. A workspot authored for one body (woman, man, big or fat man) only receives NPCs whose template suffix names that body (`_wa`, `_ma`, `_mb`, `_mf`); an NPC of unknown body only sits at a workspot with both base rigs. The clips a workspot authors for one body type only (a `workBodytypeCondition` branch) go to NPCs of that body (`clipBodies` in the catalog); an NPC of unknown body, or a body left with no clip of its own, takes any clip of the profile. What remains out is listed in `build_spot_catalog.py --report-out` and the selector's `rejected` lines.

## Settings and commands

| Convar | Default | Purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `open77_pedestrians_enabled` | `true` | Start spawning (existing NPCs retire when disabled). |
| `open77_pedestrians_buckets` | `0` | Comma-separated routing buckets eligible for spawns. |
| `open77_pedestrians_max_active` | `512` | Maximum live seated NPCs on the server, clamped to 0–512 (the platform allows 1024 NPCs per resource, shared with the walking crowd, which has its own ceiling below). |
| `open77_pedestrians_max_near_player` | `128` | Maximum live NPCs within one player's streaming radius (140 m), clamped to 0–256: a dense market can hold several hundred spots within reach. |
| `open77_pedestrians_mortal` | `true` | Shots damage pedestrians and can kill them, and a car kills a walker it hits. `false` makes them immortal: a shot takes no health and only startles them (when panic is on), and a car only knocks a walker down. |
| `open77_pedestrians_panic` | `true` | Shots and deaths make the victim and its neighbours flee and cower. `false` keeps everyone in place; a mortal pedestrian still dies in its seat. |

The two convars set the state the resource starts with (server config `convars`). Example, immortal pedestrians that never panic: `"convars": { "open77_pedestrians_mortal": "false", "open77_pedestrians_panic": "false" }`. `pedestrians.mortal on|off` and `pedestrians.panic on|off` change them while the server runs: living pedestrians take the new damage policy at once, and the next hit or death follows the new panic setting. A runtime change lasts until the resource restarts.

Restricted commands (console or the corresponding `command.pedestrians.status`, `command.pedestrians.reset`, `command.pedestrians.enable`, `command.pedestrians.mortal` or `command.pedestrians.panic` ACL grant): `pedestrians.status` reports active counts, spot cooldowns and failures, and three figures of the last scan: `slices` (how many times it paused for the next server tick: the scan is cut into slices that stay under the platform's per-tick instruction quota, so it is 0 for a few players and grows with the crowd), `groups` (players chained within 140 m of each other, in 3D, form one group) and `share` (`open77_pedestrians_max_active` divided by the groups); `pedestrians.reset` removes owned NPCs and clears cooldowns; `pedestrians.enable on|off` switches spawning at runtime; `pedestrians.mortal on|off` and `pedestrians.panic on|off` as above. The resource scans four times a second, spawns at most four NPCs per scan (16 a second, nearest first) within 120 m of a player (and 30 m above or below), streams them to clients within 140 m, and retires seated ones beyond 150 m. Vanilla authors several spots on one place for entries it never runs together; a spot stays empty while a pedestrian holds another within 0.5 m (`spotClearanceM`).

The global cap is shared between groups of players, only when it is contended. While there is room, any group takes what it can reach: one crowd may hold all of `open77_pedestrians_max_active`. Once the server is at the cap and a group under its share still has spots to fill, only groups under their share spawn, and each scan removes up to four seated pedestrians of the groups over their share (`pedestrians.status` prints `share`), the ones farthest from every player first, until those groups are down to it; the freed places go to the group that was refused. When no group is refused and a place is free, the contention ends and the free places go to whoever asks. The per-player cap `open77_pedestrians_max_near_player` applies to every client on its own.

## Shots and panic

The platform reports a hit on an Open77 NPC only to the shooter's client (`open77:npcHit`). The client script forwards it; the server accepts it for a live pedestrian in the shooter's routing bucket within 160 m of where it is and applies `hitDamage` (50 of 100 health). The victim flees from the shooter and neighbours within 15 m flee from it. Each one runs its own distance, a random 18–40 m (`fleeDistanceMinM`–`fleeDistanceM`), and a neighbour's bearing turns a random amount, at most 45° (`fleeFanDeg`), off straight away from the panic, so a group fans out instead of stopping on one line and nobody runs back past the victim. The client that holds a fleeing pedestrian's authority picks each leg on the navmesh (straight away first, then turned up to 90° or shortened, and a pedestrian cornered against a wall back along the walkway past the threat), and the other clients walk after it; a pedestrian with no reachable leg at all stays where it stands rather than being placed off the walkway. When a flee ends, the pedestrian freezes where the server last saw it and cowers there: `panicProfiles` picks the vanilla fear workspot (`cower`, kneeling with a hand on the head, twice as often as `sitafraid`, sitting hugging the knees) and a random clip of it. After a random 60–80 s it stands up, walks back to its own spot and sits there again with the clip it had; a walk that cannot get there cowers again and retries once, then the pedestrian stays where it cowers and is removed like a corpse (below). A cowering or walking pedestrian that is hit, or within 15 m of a new panic, flees again. Away from its spot it keeps its place, so no stand-in takes it. Panicked, cowering, returning and dead pedestrians are removed once no player is within 160 m of them, so nobody watches one vanish; a corpse and a pedestrian that gave up walking back would hold their place, and their watchers' share, for as long as anyone loitered in range, so they go sooner. A dead pedestrian is removed `deadRetireS` (60 s) after it died once no player is within `corpseQuietM` (25 m) of it, and `deadRetireMaxS` (120 s) after it died whatever the players do; a pedestrian that gave up walking back likewise after `strandedRetireS` (120 s) and `strandedRetireMaxS` (300 s), all four set in `config.lua`. A dead one's spot stays empty for at least 120 seconds.

That flight is the seated pedestrians': a walker's panic is the game's own crowd fear in the traffic (see Walking crowd), with no distance, fan or cowering of its own.

## Walking crowd

`server/crowd.lua` runs a second scheduler beside the seated one: civilians who walk the game's own pedestrian traffic around every ready, living player, now and then sit on a bench, a stool or at a vending machine, and panic and die together with the seated pedestrians. A hit or a death on either kind scares both within 15 m (`panicRadiusM`), and a killed walker's corpse leaves on the same timers as a seated one's.

A walker is hosted by one client and replayed exactly by the others. Its NPC is created on `tasks` with native AI, `authorityPolicy = "balanced"` and `behavior.observerReplay = true`. The platform elects as its host the nearby client that hosts the fewest NPCs (so 150 players in one place carry the walkers between them instead of one client carrying them all). Every other client retires the AI of its copy and plays the host's reported motion back at the shared server clock, so all of them show the walker in the same place. The walker spawns on a lane of the catalogue and gets one platform `traffic` task (`speed = "walk"`): its host puts the body into the game's own pedestrian traffic where it stands, and the game's native mover keeps it walking the game's lanes for good, waiting at red lights as the vanilla crowd does. The task runs until the server cancels or replaces it; it ends by itself only when the body enters one of the game's crowd workspots (below) or when the host cannot walk the body (`traffic_unavailable`). The server plans no route and holds no crosswalk light: the lanes, the lights, the animation, the ground and the avoidance of other bodies are the game's own.

A client offers to host walkers only once its player has been alive and free to move for 15 s: walkers handed to a client that had just loaded its world never got into the game's traffic.

The walkers are fully native. A body that joined the game's traffic is marked as a crowd member that the game does not release (no path found that clears the mark), and its AI no longer runs a command: a `moveTo` or a `flee` stays enqueued. So the server only swaps traffic tasks. A `traffic` task takes `speed` (`walk` or `run`), `threat` (a point) and `visits` (whether the game may send the walker to crowd workspots, default `true`); a traffic task that replaces the walker's own within one server call (the old one cancelled, then the new one enqueued) keeps the body in the traffic and applies the new values in place.

Panic is the game's own crowd fear. When a walker panics the server cancels its task (a seated walker's workspot first, giving the NPC its tasks back with `setAiMode` `tasks`) and replaces it with a traffic task at `run` whose `threat` is the shooter's position for a hit by a known player, else the point of the hit or the death. The game runs the body away from that point and refuses workspot visits meanwhile; the server picks no distance and no bearing. After `fleeTimeoutMs` (20 s) the walker calms: the server cancels the panic task and a plain walking traffic task replaces it. A walker already panicking keeps its flight.

Benches are the game's own crowd workspot visits, replayed on every client. Now and then the game sends a walker in the traffic to one of its workspots (benches and stools, but also street spots: phone calls, smoking, vending machines); when the host's body enters it the client ends the traffic task with `success` and reason `workspot` and reports the body's placement. The server takes the free spot of the crowd catalogue nearest to the body within 2 m on the ground and 3 m in height (the body is detected 0.2–1.8 m from the spot) and runs its replicated sit there: its NPC is frozen (`setAiMode` `frozen`, as the seated pedestrians') and plays a finite workspot of the spot's profile, clip and anchor for 20–60 s (`spotStayMinS`–`spotStayMaxS`), because the platform never ends a looping one, so every client plays the same clip. The host first sends the body out of the game's own workspot, and every client keeps the walker's native AI on while it plays (a workspot device does not take a body whose AI is retired). Then it gets its tasks back (`setAiMode` `tasks`), joins the traffic again where it stands, and the spot rests for 60 s (`spotCooldownS`). When the catalogue has no free spot under the body (none within reach, or it is held or resting) the walker joins the traffic with `visits = false`: the host sends it out of the game's workspot at once, so no client sees it stand where the others would see nothing, and after 60 s (`visitRetryS`) a traffic task that allows the visits replaces it in place.

A native body can get stuck: a walker in the traffic that stays within 1.5 m (`stuckM`) of one place for 60 s (`stuckS`; the game's walkers wait at red lights up to about 38 s) is removed (`stuck`); a panicking or seated walker is not judged. Any other end of a walker's traffic task (a failure, or a success for another reason than `workspot`) removes the walker too and counts the platform's reason.

Data, all generated (see below): `server/lanes-generated.lua` and `server/lanes-points-N.lua` hold the pedestrian lanes of the traffic catalogue (flag 16 clear: 18,047 lanes, 159,759 points) that walkers spawn on (each lane's points, width and class, and the metres of it where the vanilla crowd spawns) and the 227 civilian crowd records with their appearance pools. The lanes carry no connections and no traffic lights: the game's traffic walks the walker, so they only supply spawn fragments and records. `server/crowdspots-generated.lua` holds every AI spot the game's crowd may send a walker to: any workspot that has an animation profile and is not scripted scene content, with its yaw, kept only within 20 m and 3 m in height of a pedestrian lane vertex (67,789 spots, 640 profiles). The points are split over several files because a server script gets its own 1,000,000-instruction load budget; `open77.lua` lists them in order. The lanes come in two classes (1: 3–6 m wide, 12,853 lanes; 10: 2–3 m wide, 5,194 lanes).

A walker appears 35–90 m from a player (within 30 m in height), on a lane with crowd fragments, no closer than 35 m to any living player or 3 m to another walker; at most 4 a tick, one per player in turn, so a crowd of players does not starve a lone one. It leaves when no player is within 150 m, when it is stuck, or when its traffic task fails.

| Convar | Default | Purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `open77_pedestrians_crowd_enabled` | `true` | Run the walking crowd (`pedestrians.crowd off` removes it while the server runs). |
| `open77_pedestrians_crowd_max_active` | `512` | Maximum live walkers on the server, clamped to 0–512. |
| `open77_pedestrians_crowd_max_near_player` | `24` | Maximum walkers within one player's streaming radius (140 m), clamped to 0–64. |
| `open77_pedestrians_lane_classes` | `1,10` | Lane classes a walker may spawn on, a comma list. |

`mortal` and `panic` are shared with the seated pedestrians. The other constants (distances, probabilities, timers) are the `crowd` table of `server/config.lua`. `pedestrians.crowd on|off` switches the crowd; `pedestrians.reset` resets both kinds, `pedestrians.mortal` and `pedestrians.panic` apply to both, and `pedestrians.enable` acts on the seated scheduler only. After the seated lines `pedestrians.status` prints `pedestrians crowd enabled=… lanes=… points=… spots=… slices=…`, one `pedestrians crowd bucket=… walking=… seated=… panicking=… dead=…` line per bucket, `pedestrians crowd cooldowns=…` (spots resting) and one `pedestrians crowd failure=<reason> count=<n>` line per failure (`traffic` when the platform refuses a traffic task, `panic` when it refuses the panic's, `stuck`, or the platform's reason for a traffic task that ended badly, such as `traffic_unavailable`).

Both schedulers share one work budget a tick (`scanWorkPerSlice`), so together they stay within the platform's per-tick instruction quota; `slices` counts how often each paused.

## Seats players hold

`open77_seating` (world-interaction row B6) seats players on the world's furniture, often on the very spots
these pedestrians use. Three exports coordinate the two, keyed by the calling resource:

| Export | Purpose |
| --- | --- |
| `holdSeat(bucket, key, position, options?)` | Atomic: refused `npc_occupied` (with the pedestrian: `npc`, `state`, `source`, `spot`, `distance`) when a seated pedestrian, one walking back to its seat, or a corpse lies within `options.radius` (0.3–1.5 m, default 0.6) on the ground and 1 m in height; else the hold is registered or refreshed for `options.ttlMs` (1–600 s, default 30). `force` registers it anyway and returns the overlapping pedestrian. |
| `releaseSeat(bucket, key)` | Drops the caller's hold. Holds also go when they lapse or their owner stops. |
| `seatOccupants(bucket, points, radius?)` | For at most 8 points, the pedestrian holding a seat there or `false`. |

While a hold covers a spot, no seated pedestrian spawns on it, no walker's native visit sits there (refused like
an occupied spot), a pedestrian that fled it does not walk back (it keeps cowering and spends a return attempt,
so a long hold strands it and the stranded rule retires it), and one already walking back cowers where it
arrives. `pedestrians.seats` lists the holds and `returnsDiverted`.

## Limits

Loop phase is not synchronized across clients; clip identity, placement, record and appearance are. Gunfire that hits no pedestrian causes no panic. `entryActiveOnStart` is ignored. The platform allows 1024 NPCs per resource: up to 512 seated and 512 walking (shared, not reserved: a full half does not stop the other). A walker needs a host while it is streamed in: a client hosts at most 16 NPCs (the highest priority first, the rest wait for a free authority), and the balanced election gives a walker to the nearby client that hosts the fewest, so a crowd of 150 players in one place carries the 512 walkers about 3 each. A walker nobody hosts stands still until a client hosts it; the stuck clock waits meanwhile. The other clients replay the host's reported samples (interpolated, and extrapolated only briefly past the newest), so a walker is as close to the host's body as the report rate allows. Every copy walks the same walk set (the game's random `LocomotionCycle` roll is replaced on every client by one drawn from the NPC id), but the other clients play that set's plain walk or jog at the host's speed rather than the host's crowd variants: where the host shortens its steps while slowing they keep full strides, and a crosswalk jog shows as the generic jog. A car that hits a walker (`behavior.vehicleContactEnabled`) is reported by its host and confirmed by the server, which every client then plays as the same knockdown; a mortal walker dies of it, whatever the platform's contact price (10 to 90 HP of 100). A copy a client only replays falls and gets up on its own, the replay holding it meanwhile (its head watched for up to 10 s after the fall), then goes back to its host's pose. Crowd fragments ignore the time of day: walkers are as many at night as at noon, and use civilian records of the whole city rather than of the district.

## Generated catalogues

`server/spots-generated.lua`, `server/crowdspots-generated.lua`, `server/lanes-generated.lua` and `server/lanes-points-*.lua` are generated from the game's own world sectors (Phantom Liberty included) by the Open77 platform tooling, which needs a game install. Do not edit them by hand. `open77.lua` must list the lane point files in the order the generator wrote them.
